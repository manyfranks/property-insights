## Post copy

We ran the "1% rule" against 3,212 U.S. counties using Census data. Only 29 clear it.

The national median rent-to-price ratio is 0.467% monthly — a 5.6% gross yield before expenses, well short of the 1%-of-purchase-price threshold real estate investors use as a rough cash-flow screen. A few findings from the full county-level breakdown:

→ Kinney County, TX tops the list at 1.551% monthly — an 18.6% gross yield on an $82,000 median home. All 29 counties that clear 1% have median home values under $125,000; the ratio is a function of cheap housing stock, not strong rental demand.
→ Dukes County, MA (Martha's Vineyard) sits at the opposite extreme: 0.110% monthly on a $1.17M median home, a yield that only pencils if you're underwriting appreciation, not cash flow.
→ 12 of the 15 lowest-yield counties still posted double-digit 5-year price appreciation — several over 60% — despite sub-2.3% gross yields.
