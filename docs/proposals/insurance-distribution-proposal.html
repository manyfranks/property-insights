Thesis

One architecture, three terminal states, unlocked by evidence

Insurance is the highest-value vertical available to this product, and we are currently capped at roughly $25 per referral because we hold no licence. A licensed brokerage earns $414–575 gross on the same landlord policy. That gap — not the technology — is the whole strategic question.

The proposal is to build the funnel once, with a deliberately pluggable final step, so the same intake, routing, and data layer serves all three worlds: a plain affiliate link today, a pre-filled handoff to a licensed partner next, and an internal submission once a brokerage exists. Nothing gets rebuilt; only the terminal changes.

Sequencing is deliberately evidence-gated. We have zero data that property-analysis traffic converts to insurance intent. Stage 0 costs almost nothing and answers that question before any licensing, entity formation, or partner negotiation is committed.

The seam

Insurance is a vertical in the registry we already built

The affiliate registry already solves the hard part of this problem. It resolves a vendor by country, region, audience mode, and surface, with per-vendor state gating — that is precisely the machinery jurisdiction compliance needs. Insurance partners are not a new system; they are registry entries with one added capability.

What already exists and carries over unchanged

  • country + stateCoverage + stateExclusions — the exact mechanism keeping Kiavi out of its five excluded states becomes the mechanism keeping insurance CTAs out of Washington and Quebec.
  • getVendorsForSurface() — journey-mapped vertical priority, so insurance slots into the right position per page type without new routing logic.
  • FTC disclosure, per-link Sponsored labelling, Do-Not-Sell/GPC opt-out handling, and partner_clicks attribution — all inherited.

The one addition: a pluggable terminal

This is the actual moat. Every competitor funnel — The Zebra, EverQuote, SmartFinancial — opens by asking for the address and property details. We already hold most of a submission before the user types a character. It is the Matic pattern: eliminate data entry, and quote turnaround collapses from days to minutes.

Compliance expressed as code, not as a policy document

The single highest-leverage legal finding is that solicitation is defined by behaviour, not payment in most statutes. A site can be exposed even when nobody pays it. So the constraint belongs in the codebase where it cannot drift:

  • A prohibited-terms constant (best, top-rated, recommended, cheapest, compare) with a unit test that fails the build if insurance CTA copy contains them. This is what structurally prevents the Washington failure mode.
  • Line-specific exclusion lists — BC strata is not a note in a doc, it is an entry that removes the CTA.
  • Referral compensation capped in config at the safe-envelope figure, never a percentage.

Framing follows the same rule: "Build your property's coverage profile → get matched with a licensed broker", never "compare quotes." Retrofitting compliant copy onto a comparison product later is painful; starting compliant costs nothing.

Staged path

Four stages, each gated on the previous one paying for itself

Stage 0 Content modules this week · no partner needed · no PII

County landlord-insurance modules across the 3,144 county pages, using rent, value and hazard data we already hold, plus a data-backed blog post on landlord insurance cost by county. Monetised through existing affiliate CTAs.

Why first: incumbents publish state-level cost tables and stop there — county grain is unclaimed. Zero new compliance surface, no partner agreement, no personal data collected. It produces the only thing that matters right now: evidence that this traffic has insurance intent.

Stage 1 Partner slate + jurisdiction gating weeks · registry work only

Add insurance partners as registry vendors with jurisdiction gates live: Square One (already a partner, Canadian landlord), Steadily (Ambassador tier already held, US landlord), Obie (apply — best precedent for embedding with unlicensed platforms), APOLLO (Canadian embedded, 1,000+ integrations with unlicensed property managers). Encode the exclusion lists. Ship the prohibited-terms test.

Stage 2 Coverage profile + pre-filled handoff gated on Stage 0 evidence · needs privacy-policy update

The intake flow: confirm property (pre-filled), six questions, route by jurisdiction and line, hand off pre-populated. Profiles persist in Postgres.

The stored profiles are the strategic asset — a warm pipeline to bring to a broker, and hard conversion proof for negotiating equity rather than accepting a per-lead fee. Requires: a settled compensation agreement, a named routing target per jurisdiction, and a privacy-policy amendment covering lead data (we just had these pages counsel-prepped, so this is a deliberate, reviewed change).

Stage 3 Licensed structure gated on Stage 2 volume · counsel required

Canadian entity first: partner serves as Level 3 nominee, Matt holds equity, dividend mechanics disciplined from day one. Then US, which requires a US-resident licensed producer as anchor — structurally mandatory, not optional. Carrier access via one or two MGA/wholesale relationships rather than direct appointments.

The terminal step swaps from handoff to internal submission. Everything upstream is already built and proven.

Geography

Canada first, because the licensing maths is survivable there

Canada is a portfolio problem: one licensed individual can cover multiple provinces through the CISRO/CCIR Reliance Model, where the host province relies on your home-province standard instead of re-testing you. The US is a headcount problem exactly once — and the person it requires cannot be Matt.

The one structural wall

Matt cannot personally hold a US resident producer licence. Every state's home-state definition requires actual US residence or principal place of business. The Designated Home State programmes that let someone borrow Florida or Texas as a base are adjuster-only and still require citizenship or work authorisation; Georgia and Alabama require citizenship affidavits and thirteen-plus more require proof of work authorisation. A US-resident licensed partner is therefore a structural prerequisite for Stage 3 in the US, not a convenience. Canada has no equivalent wall.

Rates are drawn from primary carrier disclosures — Intact's own broker page states commissions run "5% to the high 20's"; Definity publishes Personal Property at 17.5–20%. The earlier "10–20% norm, strata ~9%" figure in our first research pass was wrong: it mixed US commercial sources with one brokerage's individually halved strata rate.

Licensing pays for itself at trivial volume

First-year licensed setup runs roughly $9,000–10,500 — courses, exams and fees (~$3,600), errors & omissions cover, entity formation and legal, annual licence fees. The delta per bound landlord policy is about $425.

Breakeven

Roughly 22–25 bound policies in year one. At a 10% referral-to-bind conversion that implies about 250 referrals; at 5%, about 500. The licensing decision is therefore not gated on cost — it is gated on whether the funnel can produce a few hundred qualified referrals a year. That is the single number Stage 0 exists to estimate.

Strata, reframed

BC banned strata referral fees because $7k–37k per building was flowing to unlicensed property managers. The ban is evidence of the size of the prize. It prohibits paying unlicensed referrers — a licensed broker placing strata earns that commission normally. Strata is simultaneously the one niche with a genuine SERP gap in Canada (no comparison experience exists) and the highest-value line available. It converts from "closed" to "the reason to get licensed" the moment a licence exists — which argues for building strata content now as a licensing-optioned asset that earns nothing today and a great deal later.

Every gate has a genuine "stop" branch. That is deliberate — the plan should be able to fail cheaply at any stage without stranded investment, which is exactly what the pluggable-terminal architecture buys.

Build now

  1. County landlord-insurance modules on the 3,144 county pages — rent, value and hazard data we hold, existing affiliate CTAs. No new compliance surface.
  2. Data-backed blog post: landlord insurance cost by county. Same hub-and-spoke pattern that the rent and tax clusters already use.
  3. Encode the exclusion lists — Washington, Quebec, New Brunswick, BC-strata — as registry gates now, before any insurance CTA ships.
  4. Prohibited-terms test that fails the build on solicitation language in insurance copy.

Risks

What could make this the wrong bet

The funnel simply doesn't convert

Most likely failure, and the cheapest to discover. Someone researching county rent statistics may be nowhere near an insurance decision. Stage 0 costs a content module and answers it. Mitigation: the module is genuinely useful SEO content regardless of whether the CTA converts, so the downside is a partial write-off, not a total one.

Partner terms cap us below the modelled economics

Flat referral fees are legally capped, and the deeper "embedded" tiers buy conversion rather than a higher rate — the research was explicit that going from a link to a pre-filled handoff improves UX and partner relationship depth, not the compensation ceiling. If partners won't move past nominal fees, Stage 2 is worth building only as a bridge to Stage 3.

The Canadian ownership question resolves badly

The dividends-versus-commission distinction rests on a New York regulatory opinion; no BC equivalent was found. If BC counsel says an unlicensed owner cannot take distributions from a brokerage whose business originates from their own website, Stage 3's economics change materially and the fallback is personal licensing under the partner's sponsorship — slower and capped at Matt's own production.

Regulatory posture tightens around lead generation

Washington's advisory and New Brunswick's channel-agnostic language both point the same direction. If more jurisdictions adopt behaviour-based tests, the unlicensed envelope narrows. This argues for treating Stage 0–2 as explicitly transitional rather than a destination, and for the copy discipline being structural rather than a style preference.

Attention cost to the core product

This is a second business grafted onto a property-analysis tool, with its own regulator, its own partners, and its own compliance burden. The staged design exists partly to keep that cost proportional to demonstrated return — but it is a real risk that insurance work crowds out the assessment product that generates the traffic in the first place.

Open questions

What needs counsel before Stage 3, and what needs deciding now

For the regulatory lawyer

  • Is there a BC ruling equivalent to NY DFS OGC 02-11-27 distinguishing dividends from commission-sharing? Highest-value Canadian question.
  • Could a genuine US entity with US-based officers establish home-state status even with no US-resident owner? Highest-value US question — if a door exists here the whole US sequence changes.
  • Does BC apply controlled-business scrutiny where nearly all business originates from one shareholder's website?
  • Does Bill 14 reach all platform compensation touching strata, including flat service fees?
  • Alberta, Ontario, Nova Scotia and Quebec referral provisions — primary-source pass required.
  • Does converting US traffic into referrals trigger entity licensing for the platform state-by-state?

For Matt, before I build

  • Confirm Stage 0 scope — county modules and blog post only, or include the BC strata hub in the first pass?
  • Is the licensed path genuinely wanted, or is this a monetisation experiment that should stay unlicensed regardless of outcome? The answer changes how much is invested in the profile/persistence layer.
  • Who is the Canadian partner broker, and are they willing to be nominee of a new entity, or only to accept referrals into their existing brokerage?
  • Is there any existing US-resident relationship that could serve as the eventual anchor, or is that a cold search?
